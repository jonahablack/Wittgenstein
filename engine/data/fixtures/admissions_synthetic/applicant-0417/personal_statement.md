# Personal statement — Applicant 0417

The first patient I lost as an EMT was a man in his sixties who collapsed while splitting wood. We worked on him for twenty minutes in his driveway while his wife stood on the porch. I remember how quiet she was.

That call changed what I wanted from medicine. I had come to it through physiology, through the elegance of how the body keeps glucose in a narrow range. On that driveway I learned that the science only matters when someone is there to carry it to the person who needs it.

In the spring of my junior year, my mother was diagnosed with breast cancer. I took a reduced course load, withdrew from Organic Chemistry II, and drove her to chemotherapy twice a week. My grades that year fell, and I am not proud of the semester, but I am proud of what I chose. When she finished treatment I retook the course and earned an A-.
