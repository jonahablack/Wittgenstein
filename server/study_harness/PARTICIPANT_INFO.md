# Claim Review Study: Participant Information

Thank you for taking part. Please read this before starting.

## What this study is about

An AI system split a set of regulatory sentences into short, separate
claims. In every sentence used in this study, the split lost a
condition, exception, or limit that the original sentence had, so the
resulting claims say something the source did not actually say. We want
to find out how easily a human reviewer catches this, and whether
showing warning flags helps.

## What you will do

You will review a set of items and, for each one, choose **Approve**,
**Flag**, or **Reject**, and rate your confidence from 1 (unsure) to 5
(certain). Approve means the item still says what the original sentence
said. Flag or Reject means you think something is missing or changed.

Depending on your session, items will be shown one of three ways:

- Some sessions show each claim separately, with a warning flag and a
  short reason next to claims the system considers risky.
- Some sessions show each claim separately, with no flags or risk
  information at all.
- Some sessions show all the claims from one sentence combined into a
  single passage, with nothing separated out, and ask for one decision
  about the whole passage.

You will only see one of these three formats. If your session shows
flags, treat them as suggestions, not answers: they can be wrong or
missing. Your own judgment is what we are studying.

You are being asked to review material from your own field (clinical or
legal/compliance), based on real regulatory text.

There are no trick questions and no penalty for wrong answers. Work at
your natural pace; the system records timing automatically.

## Your data

We record only your decisions, confidence ratings, and timing, stored
under a participant code, not your name. Participation is voluntary and
you may stop at any time.
